    How much ZEC exists, and where it's sitting

    Zcash has a fixed ceiling of 21,000,000 ZEC, full stop, the same hard cap Bitcoin made famous. Below is a live read of how much of that ceiling has been issued so far, and how it splits between public and shielded balances today.

    Halvings So Far
    2
    Next Halving (Est.)
    Late 2028
    Reward After
    0.78125 ZEC

    Every mined block adds new ZEC to the supply, and roughly every four years, about 1,680,000 blocks, that block reward gets cut in half. It happened in November 2020 and again in November 2024, taking the reward from 3.125 ZEC down to today's 1.5625 ZEC per block. The next cut triggers once the network hits block 4,406,400, which trackers currently put somewhere around late 2028, dropping the reward to 0.78125 ZEC. A halving doesn't change how Zcash works day to day. It just slows down how fast new coins enter circulation.

    There's no fixed date for the last ZEC to get mined. Zcash mirrors Bitcoin's math here: rewards keep halving on the same four-year rhythm, getting smaller each time, so issuance approaches 21,000,000 without ever technically reaching it on a fixed schedule. In practice, almost all of it will be issued within a few more decades, while the last, tiny fraction trails off far into the future.

    ◈ Why Zcash

    Money talks. On most chains, it talks to everyone.

    Every balance, every counterparty, every purchase, public and permanent, forever, on a fully transparent ledger. Zcash was built to give people a choice about that.

    ◈

    Privacy as default option

    Shielded addresses encrypt the sender, receiver, and amount on-chain. The network can still confirm the transaction is valid, it just doesn't have to show anyone the details.

    ◈

    Fungibility

    A coin with a fully exposed history can get flagged or blacklisted for who held it before you. Shielded ZEC breaks that traceable history, so a coin is worth the same no matter where it's been.

    ◈

    Selective disclosure

    A viewing key lets you share transaction details with an accountant or auditor without exposing your entire financial life to the rest of the internet.

    Mission

    Keep financial networks open and let people protect their own privacy on their own terms. That's the core of what the nonprofit Zcash Foundation works on day to day: funding protocol research, maintaining independent software like the Zebra node, and stewarding a network that stays available to anyone, not just the users of one company's product.

    Vision

    A future where financial privacy is the default, not something you have to fight for. Where sending or holding money doesn't automatically hand a permanent, searchable record to exchanges, chain analysts, or anyone else watching. Zcash treats that kind of privacy as a right worth building infrastructure for, not a luxury.

    M
    One reader's route to Zcash

    Mara's story, click to open

    +

    Mara is a composite character, not a real named individual, but the events in her story are real and documented. It's the kind of path a lot of longtime holders describe.

    Mara bought her first Bitcoin in 2013. Back then it felt like enough just to hold a wallet and write down a seed phrase. Privacy wasn't really the point. The idea was.

    Then came the losses that had nothing to do with price. In 2014, Mt. Gox, the exchange holding part of her BTC, collapsed after losing roughly 850,000 bitcoin to a hack that had gone on for years. In 2020, Ledger, the hardware wallet company she trusted with her keys, had its customer database breached. Her name, email, and home address ended up in a file leaked alongside roughly 270,000 other buyers. The phishing emails started within weeks. Some people in that same dataset later reported extortion attempts and physical threats, not because their coins were stolen, but because strangers now knew exactly who owned crypto and where to find them.

    None of that was a flaw in Bitcoin's cryptography. It was the layer underneath it: every address she'd ever used sitting in the open on a public ledger, one database leak away from being tied straight back to her name.

    That's what sent her back to the source material. Satoshi Nakamoto's original Bitcoin whitepaper actually described a system where the public could see that a payment happened without learning who was involved, identities kept separate from the transaction graph. Bitcoin's ledger never quite delivered that part. Every transaction is public and permanent, and identities get attached to it constantly, through exchanges, breaches, and plain old chain analysis.

    Zcash's original team picked that thread back up. Founded by Zooko Wilcox-O'Hearn and built on the Zerocash academic protocol using zk-SNARK zero-knowledge proofs, Zcash set out to give Bitcoin's pseudonymity the privacy Satoshi's own paper had described, but Bitcoin's design never fully delivered.

    Mara moved her long-term holdings to shielded ZEC. Not because she has anything to hide, she'll say, but because she learned, twice, what it costs when everyone else can see exactly what you hold.

    ◈ How Zcash Works

    Zero-knowledge proofs, two address types, one active pool

    Zcash uses zk-SNARKs, succinct zero-knowledge proofs, so the network can verify a shielded transaction is legitimate (right amount, no double-spend, sender actually authorized it) without ever seeing who sent what to whom.

    Transparent · t-address t1XYZ...9fQ

    Works like a Bitcoin address. Balance and transaction history are public on-chain, visible to anyone.

    Shielded · unified address u1AbC...k7M

    Today's default address format, starting with u1. A unified address bundles a shielded receiver behind one address. Amount, sender, and receiver stay encrypted, with only proof that a valid transaction happened recorded on-chain.

    One pool, built through continuous upgrades

    Ironwood is the only shielded pool accepting new deposits today. It isn't one option sitting alongside a few others, it's the current stage of a single system that's been rebuilt and hardened three times since 2016, with each upgrade closing the previous pool to new deposits and carrying its lessons forward.

    Sprout 2016 Closed

    +

    The first shielded pool. Needed a one-time trusted setup ceremony to generate its cryptographic parameters, and closed to new deposits once Sapling launched.

    Sapling 2018 Closed

    +

    Made proof generation fast enough for mobile wallets for the first time. Superseded by Orchard in 2022, though existing Sapling balances still live on-chain.

    Orchard 2022 Closed

    +

    Introduced Halo Arc, removing the need for a trusted setup entirely, and became Zcash's main shielded pool. Closed to new deposits in mid-2026 after a flaw in its proof circuit was disclosed.

    Ironwood NU6.3, 2026 Active

    The pool in use today, and the reason for that continuous line above: a formally verified circuit backed by more than 2,700 machine-checked theorems, plus a quantum-resilient design. Orchard was sealed behind a turnstile, an accounting rule that limits withdrawals to what was verifiably deposited, so a compromised pool can never be used to create coins that were never there. Anyone still holding funds in Orchard needs to move them across manually, since nothing migrates on its own.

    ◈ UX on Zcash

    What this actually looks like day to day

    The cryptography is the hard part. Using it shouldn't be.

    01

    Pick a shielded-capable wallet

    A good wallet shows your balance pool by pool, so you always know what's transparent, what's shielded, and what's still sitting in an old pool.

    02

    Choose the address type on purpose

    Send to a unified address (starting with u1) to keep the amount and both parties hidden. Send to a t-address only when you need a public record, like an exchange deposit that requires one.

    03

    Migrate old shielded funds

    ZEC left in the retired Orchard pool doesn't move to Ironwood by itself. It has to be actively sent across by the holder, wallet, or exchange holding it.

    04

    Share a viewing key, not a screenshot

    Need to prove something to an accountant or auditor? A viewing key gives read-only access to exactly the transactions you choose, nothing more.
